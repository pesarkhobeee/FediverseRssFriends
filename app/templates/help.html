📖 How rssFriends works

🌟 Your radar

Your radar is your public page at rssfriends.com/u/<your-handle>. It shows every RSS/Atom feed you subscribe to, grouped by category. Anyone can visit it (unless you set it to private).

📂 Categories & feeds

A category is a label you group feeds under — "News", "Weblogs", "GitHub", "Twitter mirrors", whatever makes sense for you. A feed is one RSS or Atom URL. You subscribe to a feed inside a category.

Go to your Dashboard to create categories and paste feed URLs into them. If you already have a reading list somewhere else, use the Import from OPML button — every RSS reader can export OPML.

🔗 Follow vs subscribe

  • Subscribe = you add a specific feed's articles to your radar. Machine-to-machine.
  • Follow = you follow a person. Their actions (stars, new subscriptions, forks) appear in your Timeline. Human-to-human.

You can subscribe to feeds without following anyone, follow people without subscribing to feeds, or both.

🔔 Follow one category

Sometimes you like someone's tech curation but not their politics. Instead of following the whole person, you can follow just one of their categories. Visit any public radar, click a category chip to filter, and hit 🔔 Follow category under the chips.

  • Only item-scoped activity flows through a category follow — their subscribes and stars from feeds in that category. Person-scoped things (they followed someone, forked a radar, cross-posted from their own feed) only reach you if you also follow the whole person.
  • Every category is also its own RSS feed at /u/<handle>/c/<category>.rss — subscribe to just that slice in any RSS reader, no rssFriends account needed.
  • Following the whole person AND one of their categories is fine — the timeline dedups events so you won't see anything twice.
  • Category follow requires view access to the radar, same as whole-user follow. Public and unlisted radars work directly; private radars need an approved follow first.

📜 Timeline vs My activity

  • Timeline — a chronological stream of what the people you follow have been doing: starring items, subscribing to feeds, forking radars, and cross-posting from their own external feeds (see below). It's a social signal, not a reader. Fresh items from your own subscribed feeds live on your dashboard and public radar.
  • My activity — your own log. Every subscribe, star, follow, fork, and cross-post you've ever done. Publicly visible unless your radar is private. On your own activity page each row has a small ✕ to delete individual events if you'd rather they didn't show — deletion propagates to your RSS feed and your followers' timelines.

🎙️ My feeds (cross-posts)

My feeds is a panel on /dashboard where you can register your own external RSS/Atom feeds — your Mastodon, your YouTube channel, your personal blog. When those feeds publish a new item, we post it to your activity as @you posted <title>, so anyone who follows you sees it on their timeline.

A few things worth knowing:

  • Only newly-discovered items count. "New" means first seen by rssFriends after you connected — not "originally published after". So a curated YouTube playlist of older videos still cross-posts (we're discovering those items for the first time). But if a feed was already in the DB before you connected (because someone else was already subscribed to it), those pre-existing items don't emit — no historical dump.
  • Capped at 20 per refresh cycle. If a brand-new heavy feed's first fetch pulls in hundreds of items, we only emit the newest 20 as cross-posts. Prevents flooding your followers on connect.
  • Attribution isn't verified. Anyone can claim any RSS URL, so each cross-post shows a source-domain chip (e.g. hachyderm.io) next to your handle. Followers see where the content came from at a glance.
  • Personal ≠ subscription. Personal feeds are about authoring; they never appear on your reading radar, in your OPML export, or in Discover's popular-feeds ranking.
  • Disconnecting doesn't delete history. Past cross-posts stay in your activity log — only future items stop being cross-posted. Individual events can still be deleted via the ✕ button on My activity.

🔭 Discover

Discover lists new radars, popular feeds, and most-forked curators — all public users only. There's a fuzzy search box at the top; type a handle (even with typos) to find someone.

🔒 Public, unlisted, private

Set from your Dashboard (Visibility dropdown):

  • Public — anyone can view your radar. Listed on Discover.
  • Unlisted — anyone with the URL can view. Not listed on Discover.
  • Private — only you and approved followers can view. Others see a "Send follow request" page.

📥📤 OPML & RSS in / out

Every user has three subscribable URLs — plug them into any RSS reader:

  • /u/<handle>.opml — the list of feeds you subscribe to, in OPML format. Import into another RSS reader to mirror your setup.
  • /u/<handle>.rss — the merged newest items across all your subscriptions, as one RSS feed. Someone can subscribe to you in NetNewsWire / Miniflux / Feedly and read your curated stream.
  • /u/<handle>/activity.rss — your Timeline-style actions (stars, subscribes) as RSS. For private users, the URL includes a signed token so only approved followers can subscribe.

🍴 Cherry-pick & fork

On someone else's radar page you'll see two shortcuts (as long as you're signed in and it's not your own):

  • Add to my radar (per-feed) — copy just that feed to your own radar, into the same category name they used.
  • Fork this radar (top of page) — copy their entire radar into yours. Duplicates are skipped, so you can fork repeatedly without piling up.

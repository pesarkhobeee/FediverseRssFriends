🌟 Your radar

Your radar is your public page at rssfriends.com/u/<your-handle>. It shows every RSS/Atom feed you subscribe to, grouped by category. Anyone can visit it (unless you set it to private).

📂 Categories & feeds

A category is a label you group feeds under — "News", "Weblogs", "GitHub", "Twitter mirrors", whatever makes sense for you. A feed is one RSS or Atom URL. You subscribe to a feed inside a category.

Go to your Settings page to create categories and paste feed URLs into them. If you already have a reading list somewhere else, use the Import from OPML button — every RSS reader can export OPML.

🔗 Follow vs subscribe

Subscribe
You add a specific feed's articles to your radar. Machine-to-machine.
Follow
You follow a person. Their bookmarks, subscriptions, and copies-of-radars appear on your Friends page. Human-to-human.

You can subscribe to feeds without following anyone, follow people without subscribing to feeds, or both.

🔔 Follow one category

Sometimes you like someone's tech curation but not their politics. Instead of following the whole person, you can follow just one of their categories. Visit any public radar, click a category chip to filter, and hit 🔔 Follow category under the chips.

  • Only item-scoped activity flows through a category follow — their subscribes and bookmarks from feeds in that category. Person-scoped things (they followed someone, copied a radar, cross-posted from their own feed) only reach you if you also follow the whole person.
  • Every category is also its own RSS feed at /u/<handle>/c/<category>.rss — subscribe to just that slice in any RSS reader, no rssFriends account needed.
  • Following the whole person AND one of their categories is fine — the timeline dedups events so you won't see anything twice.
  • Category follow requires view access to the radar, same as whole-user follow. Public and unlisted radars work directly; private radars need an approved follow first.

👥 Friends vs My activity

  • Friends — a chronological stream of what the people you follow have been doing: bookmarking items, subscribing to feeds, copying radars, and cross-posting from their own external feeds (see below). It's a social signal, not a reader. Fresh items from your own subscribed feeds live on your radar page.
  • My activity — your own log. Every subscribe, bookmark, follow, copy, and cross-post you've ever done. Publicly visible unless your radar is private. On your own activity page each row has a small ✕ to delete individual events if you'd rather they didn't show — deletion propagates to your RSS feed and your followers' Friends pages.

🎙️ My feeds (cross-posts)

My feeds is a panel on Settings where you can register your own external RSS/Atom feeds — your Mastodon, your YouTube channel, your personal blog. When those feeds publish a new item, we post it to your activity as @you posted <title>, so anyone who follows you sees it on their Friends page.

A few things worth knowing:

  • Only newly-discovered items count. "New" means first seen by rssFriends after you connected — not "originally published after". So a curated YouTube playlist of older videos still cross-posts (we're discovering those items for the first time). But if a feed was already in the DB before you connected (because someone else was already subscribed to it), those pre-existing items don't emit — no historical dump.
  • Capped at 20 per refresh cycle. If a brand-new heavy feed's first fetch pulls in hundreds of items, we only emit the newest 20 as cross-posts. Prevents flooding your followers on connect.
  • Attribution isn't verified. Anyone can claim any RSS URL, so each cross-post shows a source-domain chip (e.g. hachyderm.io) next to your handle. Followers see where the content came from at a glance.
  • Personal ≠ subscription. Personal feeds are about authoring; they never appear on your reading radar, in your OPML export, or in Discover's popular-feeds ranking.
  • Disconnecting doesn't delete history. Past cross-posts stay in your activity log — only future items stop being cross-posted. Individual events can still be deleted via the ✕ button on My activity.

🔭 Discover

Discover lists new radars, popular feeds, and most-copied curators — all public users only. There's a fuzzy search box at the top; type a handle (even with typos) to find someone.

🔒 Public, unlisted, private

Set from your Settings page (the Account panel):

  • Public — anyone can view your radar. Listed on Discover.
  • Unlisted — anyone with the URL can view. Not listed on Discover.
  • Private — only you and approved followers can view. Others see a "Send follow request" page.

📥📤 OPML & RSS in / out

Every user has three subscribable URLs — plug them into any RSS reader:

  • /u/<handle>.opml — the list of feeds you subscribe to, in OPML format. Import into another RSS reader to mirror your setup.
  • /u/<handle>.rss — the merged newest items across all your subscriptions, as one RSS feed. Someone can subscribe to you in NetNewsWire / Miniflux / Feedly and read your curated stream.
  • /u/<handle>/activity.rss — your activity feed (bookmarks, subscribes) as RSS. For private users, the URL includes a signed token so only approved followers can subscribe.

📋 Pick feeds & copy radars

On someone else's radar page you'll see two shortcuts (as long as you're signed in and it's not your own):

  • Add to my radar (per-feed) — copy just that feed to your own radar, into the same category name they used.
  • 📋 Copy this radar (top of page) — copy their entire radar into yours. Duplicates are skipped, so you can copy repeatedly without piling up.

🌐 Be followed from anywhere on the fediverse

Every public rssFriends account is a native ActivityPub actor. From Mastodon, Pleroma, another rssFriends instance, or any AP-speaking server, someone can paste @your-handle@your-rssfriends-instance into their search box and follow you.

Once someone follows you, these events land on their home timeline as regular posts:

  • Bookmarks — @you bookmarked "title" from "feed", linked to the original article.
  • Subscribing to a new feed — @you is now following "Feed Name" on rssFriends.
  • Cross-posts — items from your personal feeds (Mastodon, YouTube, blog) posted with the source-domain chip.

Nuances worth knowing:

  • The first Follow triggers a backfill — the last 20 items from your outbox are delivered to the new follower's inbox with their original timestamps, so their profile view of you isn't empty. Also re-runnable by the account owner via POST /u/<handle>/ap/backfill.
  • Erasing an event from /activity propagates — clicking the ✕ button sends a Delete{Tombstone} so the toot disappears from your followers' timelines too. Unbookmarking (☆ toggle) does NOT propagate — that's local-only by design.
  • Rename federates automatically — renaming your handle sends an AP Move activity to every remote follower. Mastodon auto-migrates the follow to your new handle (backed by the standard movedTo + alsoKnownAs handshake on both actor documents). Followers see a "moved" notice in their app and don't have to re-follow.
  • Private radars don't federate — WebFinger and the Actor endpoint both 404 for private users. Switching a public radar to private doesn't retract existing followers (that'd require a Delete{Actor}) but new follows fail.
  • This instance is currently follow-in only. You can follow accounts from Mastodon, Pleroma, Firefish or any ActivityPub server — see the next section.

🌐 Follow accounts from the open web

rssFriends is one node on the fediverse — the open network of servers that speak ActivityPub. You can follow accounts on:

  • Other rssFriends instances (self-hosted or run by friends) — @user@some-other-rssfriends.example
  • Mastodon, Pleroma, Firefish, Akkoma, GoToSocial, and anything else that implements ActivityPub actor + inbox

Their new posts land on your Friends page chronologically, mixed with local activity from the people you follow on this instance.

How to follow someone:

  1. Go to Follow from the fediverse (also linked in the site footer as 🐘 Follow from fediverse).
  2. Paste an @user@host handle (copy-paste from the target profile if unsure) and hit Follow →.
  3. The row appears as ⏳ pending. Their server confirms within a few seconds and it flips to ✓ following. Reload if you're impatient.
  4. From then on their new posts appear on your Friends timeline with a 🐘 icon and a source-domain chip.

Nuances worth knowing:

  • Historical posts don't backfill — the same Mastodon design decision that affected our outbox works both ways. You'll see posts from the moment you follow onward. Older posts remain browsable on their original server.
  • Delete propagation works — when they delete a post, we remove the cached copy from your Friends page too.
  • Unfollowing sends an Undo — clicking Unfollow posts a signed Undo{Follow} to their server and drops all their cached toots from your Friends page.
  • We don't render replies, boosts, or likes — only original Notes are shown for now. Their profile page on their own server has the full thread if you want to reply (open in a new tab from the ↗ link on the post).
  • Spam protection — we only render Notes delivered to us from actors you actively follow. Broadcast Public-addressed toots from strangers are dropped at the inbox (they'd otherwise flood your Friends page).

📚 Collections

Collections are hand-curated bundles of RSS feeds around a topic, place, or language — the "not just BBC and CNN" answer to RSS's discovery problem. Browse them at /collections, filter by language via the pill row, and either add individual feeds (☆ + button per row) or all at once ("+ Add all N feeds to my radar").

  • Two view modes: Feeds shows the curated card list (default); 🕒 Latest items gives a merged newest-first stream so you can preview what a collection actually delivers before subscribing.
  • Subscribable as RSS — every collection is also an RSS 2.0 feed at /collections/<slug>.rss. Any reader can subscribe without an rssFriends account.
  • Suggest a feed for an existing collection with "Know a feed we're missing?" at the bottom of the detail page. Curators review + accept via the Manage tab; admins get notified by email.
  • Request a new collection from the request page if the topic you want doesn't exist yet.
  • Curator removals persist across deploys — if a collection maintainer removes a feed, a tombstone is recorded so the seed loop won't quietly re-add it on the next release.

📝 Private per-feed notes

Cryptic feed titles like the_pythonist or a raw GitHub handle don't tell you who is behind them. Click the 📝 Note button on any feed row on Settings → Feeds and type a private reminder ("DHH → creator of Rails", "the_pythonist → @Samuel").

Notes appear wherever the feed shows up for you:

  • Your radar cards on /u/<you> — feeds and stream views
  • The friends timeline (/feed) byline — @dhh — creator of Rails
  • Settings → Feeds page where you edit them

Notes are strictly private — they're never rendered on other users' radars, in RSS output, or in your ActivityPub Notes. Capped at 280 characters. Per-device only in the sense that they're per-account on rssFriends — sync happens automatically across every device you sign in from.

📚 Wallabag integration

Wallabag is a self-hostable read-it-later archive (like Pocket or Instapaper, but yours). If you already run one, rssFriends can push every article you bookmark here into your Wallabag automatically — so the same star that saves it on your radar also archives the full clean text on your own instance for offline reading.

Setup (one-time, per user):

  1. On your Wallabag instance go to Developer → Create a new client. Wallabag only supports the OAuth password grant, so the redirect URI field can be anything — it isn't used. Copy the generated Client ID and Client secret.
  2. In rssFriends, open Settings → Settings, scroll to Wallabag, and paste: your instance URL, the client ID + secret you just minted, and your Wallabag username + password. Click Connect to Wallabag.
  3. That's it. Every future ★ bookmark on rssFriends fires a background task that POSTs the article's URL to your Wallabag's /api/entries.json. Your rssFriends category name is sent along as a Wallabag tag.

A few things worth knowing:

  • Best-effort dispatch. The Wallabag POST runs in the background — the ★ click stays instant. If your instance is down or the request fails, the bookmark itself is still saved on rssFriends. Failures are logged; nothing is retried later.
  • Un-bookmarking does NOT delete from Wallabag. Wallabag is your archive; rssFriends never removes entries there.
  • Credentials are encrypted at rest. Your Wallabag password, client secret, and OAuth tokens are stored in the database encrypted with a key derived from the app's session secret. A stolen database file alone doesn't reveal them.
  • Pause anytime. The Settings panel has Pause / Resume / Disconnect. Pausing keeps your tokens but stops sending; Disconnect forgets everything.
  • Same flow as KOReader + the Wallabag mobile apps — the password grant is Wallabag's officially documented and only publicly-exposed OAuth flow (docs).

📲 Install as an app

rssFriends is a Progressive Web App — you can add it to your home screen (phone) or dock (desktop) and it opens in its own window with no browser chrome, just like a native app. Same login, same account, nothing new to configure.

On iPhone / iPad (Safari)

  1. Open rssfriends.com in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button (square with an up arrow).
  3. Scroll down and tap Add to Home Screen.
  4. Confirm the name and tap Add. The RSS icon lands on your home screen.

On Android (Chrome)

  1. Open rssfriends.com in Chrome.
  2. Chrome usually shows an Install app banner near the address bar — tap it.
  3. Or use the ⋮ menu → Install app / Add to Home screen.

On macOS

  • Chrome / Edge / Brave / Arc: click the little install icon at the right end of the address bar (looks like a computer with a down-arrow), or use the ⋮ menu → Cast, save, and share → Install rssFriends…
  • Safari 17+ (macOS Sonoma): File menu → Add to Dock… → Add. The app gets its own Dock icon and window.
  • Firefox doesn't support PWA install; the closest it gets is right-click a tab → Pin Tab.

On Windows / Linux

  • Chrome / Edge: same install icon in the address bar → Install. Also in the ⋮ menu.
  • The app pins to the taskbar / applications menu with its own icon.

Why bother? A few small wins:

  • Faster repeat loads — the app caches its own static assets (CSS, icons) on your device via a service worker. Pages that need live server data (bookmarks, follows, new items) always hit the network so nothing goes stale.
  • Home-screen icon — no more digging through bookmarks. On mobile it looks and launches like any other app.
  • Own window on desktop — clean, tab-free, easy to Alt-Tab to alongside your other reader apps.
  • Offline-safe — if your connection drops the app won't try to serve you stale bookmark state; it just tells you it needs the network. Actual archival reading lives in your Wallabag integration.

Nothing to uninstall from an app store — it's just a bookmark with superpowers. Delete it any time by long-pressing the icon (mobile) or right-clicking → Uninstall (desktop).
